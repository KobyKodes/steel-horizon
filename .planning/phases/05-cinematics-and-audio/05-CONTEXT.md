# Phase 5: Cinematics & Audio - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-07-06
**Status:** Ready for research

<vision>
## How This Should Work

Firing a shot should be a cinematic, slow-paced movie moment, not a quick arcade beat. When a shot is fired, the camera follows the shell itself as it arcs from the firing ship to the target — a chase-cam feel that builds tension during the flight, then lands on a satisfying, lingering impact (splash or explosion).

When a ship is finally sunk, it should be a dramatic, gradual send-off: the ship visibly lists, takes on water, burns/smokes, and slowly slides beneath the waves over several seconds — a real emotional payoff, not a quick cut.

The aesthetic reference point is classic naval war films — booming explosions, dramatic stings, gritty smoke and fire — rather than a stylized sci-fi/energy-weapon look, even though the existing grid overlay is holographic. This tension (gritty naval war-film combat vs. the holographic teal grid already built) is worth resolving deliberately during planning rather than defaulting one way.

</vision>

<essential>
## What Must Be Nailed

- **Overall game feel/HUD as a cohesive whole** — the combination of animation, HUD polish, and audio working together matters more than any single piece being perfect in isolation. Firing and sinking sequences are the two most-repeated cinematic beats and need to feel great individually, but the phase succeeds or fails on whether it all reads as one coherent, polished experience.
- Shell-follow camera work during firing (chase-cam on the shell, not the target or the firing ship).
- Dramatic, gradual sinking sequence (list → flood/smoke → slide under), not an abrupt kill.

</essential>

<specifics>
## Specific Ideas

- Camera follows the shell in flight (not target push-in, not firing-ship push-in).
- Sinking is slow and gradual — several seconds, ship visibly lists and takes on water before sliding under.
- Naval war-film aesthetic: booming explosions, dramatic musical/audio stings, gritty smoke and fire — contrasts with the existing holographic sci-fi grid overlay. Needs a deliberate call on how these two visual languages coexist (e.g., grounded ships/effects under a stylized holographic HUD layer).

</specifics>

<notes>
## Additional Context

Existing constraint from ROADMAP.md: each firing sequence should still be ≤2.5s and skippable — this caps how "slow and cinematic" the shell-follow + impact can actually be, so the slow/dramatic feel has to be achieved through camera work and impact weight within a tight time budget, not through raw duration. Worth flagging explicitly during planning since it's in tension with "cinematic and slow."

</notes>
